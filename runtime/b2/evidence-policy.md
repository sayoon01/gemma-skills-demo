# B2 Evidence Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines when collected material is eligible to become evidence in a final research report.

It applies to every research domain.

## Evidence states

Every source candidate must be treated as one of:

- DISCOVERED
- VERIFIED
- REJECTED
- CONFLICTING

### DISCOVERED

A source found through search but not actually read.

DISCOVERED material must not be cited as final evidence.

### VERIFIED

A source that the runtime or research worker actually read and from which a directly relevant supporting or contradicting passage was extracted.

Only VERIFIED sources are eligible for final citation.

### REJECTED

A candidate source that failed verification.

Examples:

- page fetch failed;
- source was blocked;
- source did not contain the claimed information;
- citation was returned by the model but was never read;
- local page or file reference could not be confirmed.

### CONFLICTING

A verified source that materially disagrees with another verified source.

Conflicting evidence must be preserved and surfaced rather than silently removed.

## Material claim rule

A material claim is a claim that affects:

- the main conclusion;
- a technical specification;
- a product or company comparison;
- a statistic;
- a date;
- a regulatory or standards statement;
- commercialization status;
- a major trend or causal interpretation.

Material claims should be supported by at least two independent VERIFIED sources when feasible.

## Single-source rule

If exactly one credible VERIFIED source supports a material claim:

- keep the claim only if useful;
- mark it as single-source;
- do not represent it as triangulated.

## Unsupported rule

If no VERIFIED source supports a material claim:

- exclude it from the factual findings;
- or explicitly mark it as insufficient evidence.

## Independence

Different URLs from the same organization are not automatically independent sources.

Independence should primarily be evaluated by publisher or organization.

## Source quality

Prefer:

1. primary and official material;
2. standards, government and regulatory material;
3. academic research;
4. reputable independent reporting or industry analysis;
5. lower-tier secondary material only when higher-tier coverage is unavailable.

When sources conflict, higher-quality evidence should receive greater weight, but the conflict must still be recorded.

## Search snippet rule

Search snippets are never final evidence.

They may only be used to decide which source should be opened.

## Final citation rule

Every final citation must map to at least one VERIFIED evidence record.

The final synthesis model may not introduce a new URL, source, page number, or citation.
